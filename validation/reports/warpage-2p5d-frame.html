Measured warpage of a 2.5D package with a metal frame

Conclusion

The model gives a warpage of -0.1453 mm at the substrate corner at 60 °C. This is 10.3 % smaller than the moiré value of Tsai, Wang and Liu (2021), -0.162 mm. The tolerance is 13 %, the root sum square of the errors of the inputs and of the measurement (see "Tolerance"). Thus the difference is 79 % of the tolerance. The mapped mesh is converged: the 2 mm mesh changes the warpage by 0.009 % (see "Mapped mesh and convergence").

At 60 °C, the strain gauges of the paper give -0.168 mm, and the FEM of the paper gives -0.162 mm. The 2 largest errors of the budget are the zero error of the moiré (9.8 %) and the unknown die size (7.5 %).

The check is at 60 °C only. At 25 °C and 260 °C, the warpage of the model with the former 1 mm C3D10 mesh is 24 % and 22 % smaller than the moiré value (see "Other results" and "Causes of the difference"). Thus this case does not validate the warpage at these temperatures.

The 3D view shows u3, the displacement out of the plane, of the quarter package after it cools from 140 °C to 60 °C. The deformation is 50 times the true size. The package becomes a dome, and the corner moves to -z. The warpage is u3 at the corner relative to the centre. The lowest point of the substrate is not the corner, but a node on the edge x = 27.5 mm at y = 14.65 mm. It is 0.0047 mm lower than the corner.

The part and the measurement

The package is 55 x 55 x 2.73 mm. A silicon interposer of 41 x 27.5 mm holds an ASIC and 4 side dies. It is on an organic substrate, with C4 bumps in underfill UF2. Micro bumps in underfill UF1 connect the dies to the interposer. EMC fills the space at the sides of the dies, and a UF2 fillet goes around the module. A metal frame on an adhesive is on the edge of the substrate. The frame is 3.2 mm wide on 2 sides and 7.4 mm wide on the other 2 sides.

Shadow moiré with 25.4 µm/fringe measured the substrate surface from 25 to 260 °C. Back-to-back strain gauges on the diagonal measured the strains on cooling from 140 to 25 °C. The package is flat at 140 °C, and the paper uses 140 °C as the stress-free temperature. The warpage is the displacement of the substrate corner relative to the centre.

Reference

The reference is -0.162 mm, the moiré value at 60 °C in Figure 20. At the same temperature the strain gauges give -0.168 mm, and the FEM of the paper gives -0.162 mm. The sign follows from the model axes. On cooling, the substrate contracts more than the silicon, so the package becomes a dome and the corner moves to -z.

The check uses 60 °C because there, Table 1 gives all inputs with no choice at a Tg. Of the measured temperatures with this property, 60 °C has the largest warpage. At 25 °C the adhesive crosses its Tg. The paper does not tell how its FEM uses the 2 values of the adhesive, and this choice changes the result by 22%. Causes of the difference

  1. At 25 °C the adhesive is below its Tg, and Table 1 gives E = 100 MPa. The frame then holds the substrate more. This gives a corner that is 7 µm higher than the lowest point, and a warpage 24% below the moiré value. With the adhesive at E = 20 MPa at all temperatures, the warpage is -209.5 µm (-7.0% to the moiré), k2 is +10% and k3 is +14% to the FEM of the paper. In Figures 18 and 20, the FEM of the paper is a straight line from 25 to 140 °C, and the measurements show no change of slope at 50 °C. Thus the FEM of the paper possibly does not use the change of the adhesive at its Tg. A viscoelastic adhesive can also stay soft below its Tg for the time of the test.
  2. The paper gives no die size. A die block that fills the module gives 7.5% more warpage at 60 °C and 8.5% more at 25 °C.
  3. At 260 °C the value of femahoy is 2.7% above the FEM of the paper, but both models are about 22% below the moiré value. The 2 linear elastic models with the data of Table 1 agree, so the cause is in the data or in the model type, not in the solve. Above 140 °C the EMC and the underfills are above their Tg, where viscoelastic behaviour is usually large.
  4. The moiré gives -15.8 µm at the stress-free temperature. This zero error is about 10% of the warpage at 60 °C.
  5. The frame has a large effect: with no frame, the warpage at 25 °C is -344 µm. Thus the warpage is sensitive to each input of the frame and the adhesive.

Mapped mesh and convergence

mesh.py writes a tensor grid through every material boundary. Adjacent blocks share corner nodes and middle nodes. Each block has C3D20R elements, with 4 elements through the substrate and at least 1 through each thin layer. Each interval uses max(1, ceil(length / h - 1e-9)) elements, except the substrate thickness. This grid matches the audit mesh and the transfinite box rule in gmsh. The UF2 fillet has 3 steps, each 0.3 mm wide. The midpoint of each third sets its height. The grid preserves the material sets, symmetry sets, ORIGIN and CORNER.

Reason: The audit shows a 1.6 % mesh error in the former case. The mapped mesh gives a converged result with 5459 nodes.

Reproduce

Run python3 mesh.py to write the case mesh at h = 6 mm. Run python3 mesh.py H to write another mesh, with H = 4 or 2 for the table. Run fh validate --root ROOT with a copy of this case under ROOT/validation/cases. The script needs only the Python standard library. The material data and the step stay in job.inp.

Limits

The paper has 3 more models:

  1. A 3D quarter model of a flip-chip package. It is not a case, so that the suite stays small. With the same method and a 0.5 mm mesh, femahoy gives -58.3 µm at 25 °C and 28.4 µm at 260 °C. The moiré values of Figure 9 are -61.2 and 31.0 µm, and the FEM values of the paper are -58.1 and 28.8 µm.
  2. 2D plane stress and axisymmetric beam models for Figures 6 to 8. The paper gives no geometry or materials for them.
  3. A 2D axisymmetric model of the flip-chip package for Figures 12 and 13. fh mesh makes no axisymmetric elements.
