This case checks a submodel: a fine model of the region at a hole that gets its displacement on the cut boundary from a coarse global model (units SI-mm). The coarse global model alone gives the peak stress at the hole 4.5 % low. The submodel gives it 0.14 % high, and 0.029 % high at the integration point of the check.

Source

G. Kirsch, "Die Theorie der Elastizität und die Bedürfnisse der Festigkeitslehre", Zeitschrift des Vereines deutscher Ingenieure 42 (1898) 797-807: a circular hole in an infinite plate in tension. The solution is also in S. P. Timoshenko and J. N. Goodier, Theory of Elasticity, 3rd ed., McGraw-Hill, 1970, article 35. For the tension σ along x and a hole of radius a, in polar coordinates r, θ:

  • σ_rr = σ/2 (1 - a²/r²) + σ/2 (1 - 4a²/r² + 3a⁴/r⁴) cos 2θ
  • σ_θθ = σ/2 (1 + a²/r²) - σ/2 (1 + 3a⁴/r⁴) cos 2θ
  • σ_rθ = -σ/2 (1 + 2a²/r² - 3a⁴/r⁴) sin 2θ

At the top of the hole, (0, a), σ_xx = σ_θθ = 3σ. The displacement of this stress field, with μ the shear modulus and κ = 3 - 4ν in plane strain, is:

  • u_x = σ a / (8μ) [(r/a)(κ + 1) cos θ + 2 (a/r)((1 + κ) cos θ + cos 3θ) - 2 (a/r)³ cos 3θ]
  • u_y = σ a / (8μ) [(r/a)(κ - 3) sin θ + 2 (a/r)((1 - κ) sin θ + sin 3θ) - 2 (a/r)³ sin 3θ]

The strains of this displacement give the 3 stresses above. A check with central differences at 6 points gave a difference of less than 10⁻⁷ MPa.

In plane strain and in plane stress, the in-plane stresses are the same. Only the displacement changes (κ = (3 - ν)/(1 + ν) in plane stress).

The submodel in CalculiX

job.inp has these lines:

*SUBMODEL, TYPE=NODE, INPUT=global.frd
CUT
...
*BOUNDARY, SUBMODEL, STEP=1
CUT, 1, 2

ccx reads the result file global.frd of the global model, finds the global element that holds each node of CUT, and interpolates the displacement of step 1 at the node. The submodel has no other load.

ccx reads global.frd in the folder where it runs. Thus fh copies global.inp (the file with the name of global.frd and the extension .inp) and its *INCLUDE files with job.inp, and solves global.inp first in the same folder. global.inp writes *NODE FILE, OUTPUT=3D. ccx makes a 3D element of each 2D element, and the submodel finds its nodes in the 3D elements of global.frd. With the 2D elements of the default output, the submodel stopped with *ERROR in dsort: the number of values to be sorted is not positive.

The check

CalculiX writes the stress only at the integration points (*EL PRINT). Thus the check compares with the exact stress at an integration point. TOP is the element of the submodel at the hole next to (0, a). The check is the largest σ_xx of its integration points. mesh.py finds the 2 x 2 integration points of TOP (±1/√3 in the element) and prints the Kirsch σ_xx at the point nearest (0, a): x = 0.139 mm, y = 10.052 mm, σ_xx = 296.179 MPa.

The result is σ_xx = 296.265 MPa, 0.029 % above the reference. The nodal value of S11 at (0, a) in job.frd (the mean of the elements at the node) is 300.41 MPa, 0.14 % above 3σ. The global model alone gives 286.51 MPa at (0, a) in global.frd, 4.5 % below 3σ.

curves.py writes σ_xx on the line x = 0 of the 3 solutions (the nodal S11 of global.frd and job.frd of the run) to the curve line-x0.csv of the report. At y = 12.46 mm, the second node of the global model, the global model gives 8 % above Kirsch. From the hole to y = 18 mm, the submodel stays within 0.4 % of Kirsch at its nodes on the line. Near the cut, the submodel has the error of the global model: -1.0 % at y = 19.1 mm and -3.3 % at the cut, y = 20 mm. Thus read the results of a submodel away from its cut.

Tolerance

The tolerance is 0.1 % (rel_tol = 0.001). It is the sum of the error of the global displacement on the cut (0.03 %) and the error of the submodel mesh (0.001 %), with a margin of 3 times. An error of the method is larger: a cut at R = 1.5a gives -1.0 %, a global model with 6 elements gives -1.7 %, and the coarse global model alone gives -4.5 % at the node.
