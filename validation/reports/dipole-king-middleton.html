This case checks the antenna path: the input impedance and the far field of a centre-fed dipole, solved with openEMS (FDTD). The impedance reference is the published second-order theory of King and Middleton. The directivity reference is analytical. The tolerance comes from the mesh error and from the difference between the strip of the model and the round dipole of the reference.

Why this case

No other case of the suite has any of these items:

  • High-frequency electromagnetics in the time domain: the FDTD solver of openEMS.
  • Radiation into free space, with a perfectly matched layer (PML) on the 6 faces.
  • A lumped port, and 1 pulse that gives the input impedance and S11 over a broad band (0.4 to 2.8 GHz, a ratio of 7).
  • A near-field to far-field transformation: the directivity and the radiated power.

The dipole is the simplest antenna with a published impedance, and its far field has an analytical form.

Reference

  • R. W. P. King, "The Theory of Linear Antennas", Harvard University Press, 1956, Tables 30.6 to 30.10 (pages 172 to 176). The tables give the second-order impedance of King and Middleton for a centre-fed tube of radius a and half length h, with a delta-gap feed. The values are a function of beta h = 2 pi h / wavelength and of the thickness parameter Omega = 2 ln(2h / a). The values below are copied from the comparison of AN-SOF with these tables, https://www.antennasimulator.com/download/AN-SOF_vs_King-Middleton_2nd_Order_Impedances.xlsx. The same file gives the solution of AN-SOF, a method-of-moments program, for the same dipoles. This case uses it only to estimate the error of the second-order theory.
  • C. A. Balanis, "Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design", 4th edition, Wiley, 2016, section 4.6, "Half-wavelength dipole". A sinusoidal current on a dipole of length lambda / 2 gives the directivity D0 = 4 / Cin(2 pi) = 1.641, with Cin(2 pi) = 2.4377.

This case uses Omega = 12.5, thus 2h / a = 518. For this Omega, the theory of King and Middleton and the method of moments agree to 2 % or better at beta h = 1.2 to 1.5:

Model

The openEMS case is the folder omega12.5: openems.py for the Python modules of openEMS 0.37.0. The lengths are in mm.

ItemValue
Dipolea strip of metal with no thickness, width w = 1.2 mm, along z in the plane y = 0. Half length h = 77.70 mm, thus Omega = 2 ln(2h / a) = 12.5 with a = w / 4 = 0.3 mm
Equivalent radiusa strip of width w acts as a round wire of radius w / 4 (C. M. Butler, "The equivalent radius of a narrow conducting strip", IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation 30(4), 1982, pages 755 to 758)
Feeda gap of 0.6 mm at the centre, with a lumped port of 50 ohm across the full width
PulseGaussian, 1.5 GHz +/- 1.3 GHz. The run stops when the energy is 60 dB below its peak
Free space100 mm of air on each side of the dipole (a quarter wavelength at beta h = 1.2), then a PML of 8 cells
Mesh4 cells across the strip (d = 0.3 mm), with a line d/3 in the metal and the next line 2d/3 out of the metal at each edge and each end. The cells grow by 1.3 times or less, to a twentieth of the wavelength at 2.8 GHz. 405130 cells
Far fielda box of near-field to far-field transformation at the frequency of beta h = pi / 2, then the far field on the full sphere in steps of 2 degrees (theta) and 5 degrees (phi)
Outputdipole.txt: 1 row with the checks and with R12, X17, the resonance bh_res and R_res. impedance.txt: R, X and S11 for 50 ohm from 0.4 to 2.8 GHz. pattern.pos: the far-field pattern at beta h = pi / 2, a surface at the distance D(theta, phi) from the centre, for the viewer

The impedance is a function of beta h and Omega only. Thus the band of the pulse gives the values at each beta h of the table with 1 dipole.

Problems that the model solves:

  • A sheet edge on a mesh line acts as an edge about d/3 out of the metal. Thus a strip with 4 cells across its width acts as a wider strip, and X12 has an error of 5 %. The 1/3 rule of the openEMS documentation puts the effective edge at the true edge.
  • With the end at 50 dB below the peak, R has a ripple of +/- 10 ohm below 0.6 GHz: the run stops before the current at low frequency is small. At 60 dB, R is smooth from 0.4 GHz. The checks change by 0.5 % or less.
  • Run changes the working folder of Python to the simulation folder. The script changes it back before it reads the results.

Not in the check

  • S11: it is a function of Z and of the port resistance, (Z - 50) / (Z + 50). impedance.txt gives it for the page.
  • The second resonance and the antiresonance near beta h = 2.7 are in impedance.txt. The reference table stops at beta h = 1.7.
  • A ground plane, a dielectric or a patch antenna: openEMS solves them with the same port and far-field tools. A published measurement with a clear geometry is the next case for these.
