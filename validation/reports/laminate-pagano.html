Made by scripts/validation-reports.py on 2026-10-23, femahoy 3493b3a.

This case checks the 2 CalculiX models of a composite laminate against the exact 3D elasticity solution of Pagano (units SI-mm):

  • THIN: composite shells (*SHELL SECTION, COMPOSITE, S8R), a/h = 100.
  • THICK: 3D layers (C3D20R, 3 elements in each ply, 1 *ORIENTATION for each ply angle), a/h = 4.

Both plates use the ply material *ELASTIC, TYPE=ENGINEERING CONSTANTS, and the checks read the stresses with *EL PRINT, GLOBAL=YES.

Source

N. J. Pagano, "Exact solutions for rectangular bidirectional composites and sandwich plates", Journal of Composite Materials 4 (1970) 20-34. The problem is a square plate of side a and thickness h, simply supported on the 4 edges, with the load q = q0 sin(πx/a) sin(πy/a) on the top face. The plate has 3 plies of h/3 at 0, 90 and 0 degrees. The ply has E_L/E_T = 25, G_LT/E_T = 0.5, G_TT/E_T = 0.2 and ν_LT = ν_TT = 0.25 (L is the fibre).

For this load, each field is 1 sine or cosine term in x and y, times a function of z. In each ply, the 3D equations of elasticity then become 6 linear equations in z with constant coefficients. pagano.py solves them exactly: the state (U, V, W, τ_xz, τ_yz, σ_z) goes from the bottom face to the top face through the matrix exponential of each ply. The displacements and the transverse stresses are continuous at each interface. The top and bottom faces have τ_xz = τ_yz = 0, and σ_z = -q0 on the top and 0 on the bottom. This is the solution of Pagano. pagano.py has no other approximation than the round-off and the truncation of the series of the exponential (below 1e-12).

Model

Each plate is 1/4 of the plate, with symmetry on x = a/2 and y = a/2. In local x (x - 60 for THICK):

SetWhereCondition
*_SSXx = 0v = w = 0 (simple support)
*_SSYy = 0u = w = 0 (simple support)
*_SYMXx = a/2u = 0, and for the shell the rotation about y (DOF 5)
*_SYMYy = a/2v = 0, and for the shell the rotation about x (DOF 4)

The simple support of THICK is the support of Pagano: v = w = 0 on the full face x = 0, and σ_xx = 0 there. Without DOF 5 and 4 on the symmetry edges, the shell is free to turn there, and w of THIN was 15 times too large.

  • THIN: 8 x 8 S8R. *SHELL SECTION, COMPOSITE has 1 line for each layer: the thickness, an empty field, the material and the orientation. The normal of the elements is +z, thus the first layer is at the bottom.
  • THICK: 8 x 8 x 9 C3D20R. The element sets THICK_0 and THICK_90 have their own *SOLID SECTION and orientation.
  • OR0 puts the fibre (axis 1) on x, OR90 puts it on y.
  • Load: pagano.py writes the nodal forces of q0 sin sin, from the shape functions of the 8-node faces with 4 x 4 Gauss points (load.inp). For THIN they are on the nodes of the shells, thus on the mid-surface. For THICK they are on the top face.

Reference values

CalculiX writes the stress only at the integration points (*EL PRINT), not at the faces of the plate. Thus each stress check compares with the exact stress at the integration points of its element set. pagano.py computes them from the positions of the 2 x 2 x 2 Gauss points (±1/√3) of each element. A composite shell has 2 points across each layer.

τ_xz is negative at x = 0, thus the check takes the mean, not the maximum. GLOBAL=YES gives the stresses in x, y, z. Without it, ccx gives them in the axes of the ply, and it adds the name of the orientation at the end of each line.

Composite shell and the span ratio

python3 pagano.py --thin-h H makes THIN H mm thick. Then the 3 layer thicknesses in job.inp must be H/3. With 8 x 8 S8R:

The composite shell is too stiff in transverse shear, because 1 brick cannot follow the zigzag of the displacement across the plies. Thus use composite shells only for a/h of 50 or more with this material (E_L/G_TT = 125). Use 3D layers for a thicker laminate. The transverse shear stress of a composite shell comes from the strain of the brick, not from the equilibrium. At a/h = 100, τ_xz at the bottom points of the shell is 3 times the exact value, and in the core it is 1/3 of the exact value. A test with S6 (each S8R cut into 2 S6) gave w of THIN -0.42 %.

Tolerance

The tolerance is 0.5 % (rel_tol = 0.005) for each check. The largest errors are the model error of the shell (0.17 %) and the mesh error of sx_thick (0.17 %). The exact values have an error below 0.001 %. The remaining margin is for a change of the solver version. A wrong layup or a wrong ply constant gives an error of 10 % or more (see Result).
